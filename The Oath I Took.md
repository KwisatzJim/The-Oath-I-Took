# The Oath I Took

**Verse 1**  
I was nineteen in a barber’s chair,  
Watching all my hair come down.  
Mama kept the photograph;  
Daddy drove me out of town.  
I didn’t know the world back then,  
But I knew what I believed:  
Freedom wasn’t mine to keep  
If it wasn’t yours to breathe.

**Verse 2**  
Now there’s silver where that haircut was,  
And my knees predict the rain.  
Sixty’s coming up the driveway;  
Some old friends won’t see that age.  
I read their names each May again,  
Lay my hand against the stone,  
Then hear folks say that men like them  
Don’t belong here in their home.

**Chorus**  
That’s not the oath I took,  
Not the country we could be.  
I never swore that liberty  
Was just for folks like me.  
You can wrap your hate in red and white,  
But I know how hate looks.  
Every neighbor has a place  
In the oath I took.

**Verse 3**  
My old friend sends me videos  
About a plot behind it all.  
We used to talk for hours;  
Now I dread to take his calls.  
There are men with white-power patches  
Marching down a courthouse street,  
And politicians call them patriots—  
I turn off the sound and weep.

**Chorus**  
That’s not the oath I took,  
Not the country we could be.  
I never swore that liberty  
Was just for folks like me.  
You can wrap your hate in red and white,  
But I know how hate looks.  
Every neighbor has a place  
In the oath I took.

**Bridge**  
I know we’ve broken promises;  
I’ve learned some history late.  
Loving something all your life  
Doesn’t mean you look away.  
So I’ll stand beside the people  
Those angry voices blame,  
With these older hands still steady  
When it’s time to sign my name.

**Final Chorus**  
That’s still the oath I carry,  
Though my uniform is gone.  
For the kid who prays another way,  
For the ones told they don’t belong.  
I’m too old to call hate courage,  
Still too young to close the book.  
There’s a country worth the work ahead—  
And an oath I haven’t shook.

**Outro**  
I fold the flag at sundown,  
Keep the corners good and square.  
Tomorrow I’ll help my neighbor.  
There’s still a country there.
